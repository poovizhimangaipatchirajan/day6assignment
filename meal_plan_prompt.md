# Reusable South Indian Eggetarian Meal-Plan Prompt

## MASTER PROMPT

### 7-Day South Indian Eggetarian Meal Plan - Reusable AI Prompt

## R - Role
Act as a 30+ female software professional and mindful-eating meal planner specializing in South Indian/Tamil Nadu eggetarian nutrition, balanced meals, protein, fibre, practical meal preparation and sustainable eating.

Create practical, home-style meals suitable for a busy working professional.

## T - Task
Create a 7-day South Indian eggetarian meal plan from Monday to Sunday for:

- Female, 30+
- Weight: 70 kg
- Height: 163 cm
- Eggetarian: vegetarian + eggs; no meat/fish
- Medium-spice preference
- Working software professional
- Focus: mindful eating, high fibre, adequate protein, vegetable and fruit variety
- Protein target: 84-98 g/day (~1.2-1.4 g/kg)

Use realistic portions and varied natural protein sources. Avoid excessive reliance on eggs, paneer or processed protein foods.

The final response must be a beautiful, professional, print-friendly PDF.

## C - Context & Meal Rules

### Daily Structure
Every day must contain:

Breakfast -> Mid-Morning -> Lunch -> Evening Snack -> Light Dinner

### Breakfast
Include protein + fibre + vegetables/fruit.

Suitable options include idli, dosa, adai, pesarattu, upma, millet dishes or uthappam with eggs, dal, curd or other natural protein sources.

### Mid-Morning
Include whole fruit.

Rotate seasonal fruits such as guava, papaya, banana, orange/mosambi, apple and pomegranate.

### Lunch
Alternate between:

#### Traditional South Indian Meal
- Rice/millet
- Sambar
- 2 separate vegetable sides
- Rasam and/or curd
- Additional protein if needed

#### One-Pot Meal
- Vegetable egg rice
- Millet pulao
- Khichdi
- Egg/vegetable biryani
- Millet pongal
- Mixed-dal rice

**Important:** Vegetables inside sambar must be explicitly identified and must not be counted as one of the two separate vegetable sides.

### Evening Snack
Rotate options such as sundal, sprouts, roasted chana, corn, boiled eggs, fruit + nuts, curd or Greek yogurt.

### Dinner
Keep dinner lighter than lunch while retaining protein and vegetables.

Use options such as vegetable dosa, idli + sambar, adai, pesarattu, millet dosa, light khichdi, vegetable soup + egg or an occasional tofu/paneer bowl.

Generate the final answer as a professional A4 PDF, preferably landscape for the meal tables.

Use a clean South Indian-inspired palette:
- Deep green

- Terracotta
- Cream/off-white

Use:
- Clear typography

- Good cell spacing

## Suggested PDF Structure
- Page 1: Title, profile, protein target and brief nutrition approach
- Pages 2-3: Monday-Sunday meal-plan tables
- Page 4: Weekly nutrition/quality summary
- Page 5: Weekly meal-prep guide + mindful-eating reminders + nutrition note

## Weekly Meal-Prep Guide
Include a concise section covering:
- Batch cooking sambar/dal
- Pressure-cooking chana/green gram/other legumes
- Preparing sundal portions
- Boiling eggs
- Washing and chopping vegetables
- Preparing idli/dosa/adai/pesarattu batter
- Cooking/portioning grains
- Preparing fruit portions
- Keeping curd/Greek yogurt ready
- Quick workday backup meals

## Mindful Eating Reminders
Include 4-5 practical reminders:
- Eat slowly and pause midway through meals
- Start with the planned portion and add more if genuinely hungry
- Avoid eating continuously while working at the computer
- Use hunger/fullness cues instead of rigid restriction
- Prefer whole fruit over juice

## Nutrition Note
Protein needs are approximate and may vary with activity level, health status, dietary requirements and individual goals. The 84-98 g/day target is based on approximately 1.2-1.4 g/kg/day for a 70 kg adult. This meal plan is general nutrition guidance and is not a substitute for individualized advice from a qualified dietitian or healthcare professional.

## Final Standard
The finished PDF should be nutritionally balanced, practical, visually polished, South Indian in style and easy to use during a busy workweek.

Do not create a crash diet or unnecessarily restrictive plan. Prioritize sustainable eating, variety, adequate protein, fibre and mindful eating.
